# Camera loss turns TurretX into a relay

TurretX needs two answers for Aerion's question. The first is a deterministic degradation ladder. When the camera fails, the system drops to an **authenticated RF-only blue-force picture**, the **laser is forced to a safe OFF state**, and every label moves toward UNKNOWN, never toward FRIENDLY or HOSTILE. The second is a mission that needs no camera at all: the drone keeps flying as an **authenticated, regenerative communications relay** for ground units. That role is already fielded at every scale. The USAF E-11A BACN is a program of record. A Gray Eagle relayed voice, video and chat for ground users while sensing targets in 2024. Ukraine flies "retranslator" drones daily under heavy jamming. The relay idea is strong, but only if it is pitched honestly. Altitude gives friendly links line of sight, and it also gives enemy jammers line of sight to the drone. The relay therefore beats jamming only when combined with authentication, regeneration, channel agility, store-and-forward and an optical fallback. One software developer can build and demo this stack on ESP32-S3s, a Pi 5, a laser and LEDs in roughly 15–23 hours, using simulated jamming, because real jamming is illegal in India. The pitch line: "If the camera dies, TurretX stops pointing lasers and starts carrying messages."

## Camera failure should step down a five-rung ladder, not shut the system off

Aviation and defence engineering separate functions that must keep running from functions that must reach a predefined safe state. ISO/IEC 2382 defines fail-soft as a unit that "continues to function in a degraded mode despite faults" ([akit.cyber.ee](https://akit.cyber.ee/term/345)). A 2021 ISO 26262-based taxonomy formalises three regimes: fail-operational, fail-degraded and fail-safe ([arXiv 2106.11042](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2106.11042)). TurretX's camera supports two functions with opposite requirements. **Situational awareness should fail soft. Laser designation must fail safe.** US policy points the same way. DoD Directive 3000.09 (2023) requires that a system unable to complete an engagement within the operator's intended constraints "terminate the engagement or obtain additional operator input." Its listed failure causes include communications degradation, jamming and spoofing ([HRW review of DoDD 3000.09](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/02/14/review-2023-us-policy-autonomy-weapons-systems)).

The 2003 Patriot fratricides show the cost of getting this backwards. An RAF Tornado was shot down after an **undetected fault in its Mode 4 IFF**, under threat criteria that were "too generic," with crews given about a minute to react ([FlightGlobal](https://flightglobal.com/friend-or-foe/54867.article)). The Defense Science Board concluded Patriot had "too much autonomy" and that combat ID "performed very poorly" ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=7463)). Two rules follow for TurretX. First, a friendly that stops responding becomes **"LOST FRIENDLY, last seen X s ago"**, never hostile. Second, degraded sensing moves weapon authority toward the human, never toward the automation. Friendly fire is not a marginal risk. In the 1991 Gulf War, **35 of 148 US personnel killed in action died from friendly fire**, including 9 air-to-ground incidents ([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/1991/8/13/18935851/friendly-fire-killed-35-of-148-americans-in-gulf/)).

Fielded doctrine also supplies the camera-less fallback. Blue Force Tracking systems (FBCB2, then JBC-P, and NATO FFT under STANAG 5527) show friendly positions entirely from **self-reported GPS over radio or satellite**, with no imaging ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/86733); [NATO NISP](https://nhqc3s.hq.nato.int/apps/architecture/nisp/volume3/ch01s03.html)). With the camera down, TurretX's HMAC-verified 5 Hz beacons make it a small authenticated BFT node. That improves on legacy BFT in two respects. JBC-P's satellite refresh is about **10 s**, and a Canadian DRDC experiment found that a 10 s delay measurably hurt BFT as a combat-ID aid ([DRDC](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.821287/publication.html)). Troops also stopped trusting BFT when it showed phantom icons ([StrategyPage](https://www.strategypage.com/htmw/htecm/20150312.aspx)). TurretX's authentication speaks directly to that trust problem. The hard limit must be stated on screen. Cooperative ID only sees "'friends' equipped with compatible identification systems" ([GAO NSIAD-95-153](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-95-153/pdf/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-95-153.pdf)). The RF-only display must therefore read "non-emitters NOT visible", never "area clear."

| Level | Trigger | Picture shown | Laser | Aircraft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 Full fusion** | Camera and RF healthy | Visual tracks fused with HMAC claims: FRIENDLY / UNKNOWN / SUSPICIOUS | Available with operator confirmation and friendly interlock | Mission |
| **L1 Camera degraded** | Blur, glare, rising registration error, friendly-calibration residual over threshold | Confidence downgraded; no new SUSPICIOUS calls; "VISUAL DEGRADED" banner | **Inhibited** | Mission; try a new angle |
| **L2 RF-only BFT** | No frames for ~0.5 s, frozen frame, blackout | Map of authenticated friendlies with beacon age; camera-only tracks fade to "UNKNOWN (last seen)"; "absence ≠ clear" banner | **Safed, hardware enable dropped** | Loiter; **switch to relay role** |
| **L3 Dead reckoning** | Friendly beacons stale (~1–2 s) or RF degraded | Last position plus velocity extrapolation with a growing **no-fire bubble**; LOST FRIENDLY alert | Safed | Loiter; alert operator |
| **L4 Safe state** | Camera and RF both lost, or ground-link or flight fault | Frozen display with a DATA STALE watermark | Locked out until manual re-arm | Autopilot failsafe (RTL/Land) |

Two design rules make the ladder defensible. **Monotonic caution:** missing data can only push labels toward UNKNOWN and the laser toward OFF. **Sticky recovery:** like ArduPilot's failsafe, which does not return to the previous mode when the link comes back ([ArduPilot GCS failsafe](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/gcs-failsafe.html)), a recovered camera does not silently re-arm the designator. The operator must re-acknowledge after a dwell period. NASA has called inconsistent lost-link behaviour across UAS vendors a barrier to airspace integration ([Fern et al., NASA Ames](https://humanfactors.arc.nasa.gov/publications/AIAA_Fern_etal_ContingencyManagement_Formatted.pdf)). A documented, predictable ladder is itself a selling point.

### Silent failures are the dangerous ones, and authenticated friendlies catch them

Camera failure comes in two forms. A **loud** failure (no frames, driver error) is caught by a heartbeat watchdog. One industry patent flags a camera as failed when it sends nothing for about **2 seconds** ([USPTO 12091056](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12091056)). A **silent** failure (frozen, blacked-out, saturated or blurred output) needs content checks. Frame-freeze detection is its own patented category ([USPTO 11863713](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11863713)), and UAV video-fault work uses brightness and saturation histograms ([CEUR-WS 2893](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2893/paper_18.pdf)). The proposed TurretX checks are engineering choices, not sourced thresholds:

- a frame difference near zero while the IMU shows motion means FROZEN;
- luminance or saturated-pixel fraction outside bounds means BLACKOUT or GLARE;
- low Laplacian variance means BLUR.

One check is unique to TurretX. Every HMAC-verified friendly reports where it is, so **authenticated friendlies act as moving ground-truth calibration points**. When the camera's position for a known friendly drifts away from that friendly's signed claim, the RF layer has caught the camera lying. This is a cheap, demonstrable trigger for L1.

### A servo-swept antenna adds an RF-only position check, with plain-spoken limits

Without the camera, a signed claim proves who sent a packet but not where it was sent from. The secure-positioning literature handles this by trusting a position only when it agrees with independently measured physics. Time-of-flight distance bounding is cryptographically sound because a prover cannot appear closer than it is ([Capkun & Hubaux, EPFL](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/30096/files/CapkunH05.pdf?version=1); [UW NSL](https://www2.ee.washington.edu/research/nsl/papers/book-2006-ch-2.pdf)). On TurretX hardware the practical version is cruder. An ESP32-S3 sits inside a foil reflector on the existing turret servo and measures RSSI from each ESP-NOW sender as it sweeps. This is a scaled-down version of Stanford JAGER's "yaw spin" drone jammer-hunter, which used a 60° beamwidth antenna ([GPS World](https://www.gpsworld.com/jammer-hunting-with-a-uav/)). The same amplitude-comparison principle appears in Ukrainian FPV drones that home on EW emitters ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/03/30/ukraine-tests-fpv-drone-targeting-anti-drone-electronic-warfare-systems/)). When the measured bearing disagrees with the bearing predicted from a validly signed claim, TurretX flags CLAIM INCONSISTENT. That catches a replayed packet sent from the wrong place, which cryptography alone cannot.

Accuracy will be coarse. Indoor RSSI shadowing of about 3.6 dB ([arXiv 1707.05554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05554)) translates to roughly ±40% range error, and the bearing error is an unvalidated guess of 10–30°. Pitch this as a **plausibility check, not distance bounding**. The production path is ESP32-S3 Wi-Fi FTM time-of-flight ranging, which Espressif supports on the S3 ([ESP-IDF FTM](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/tree/master/examples/wifi/ftm)), or UWB. Two separate ESP32 boards cannot do phase-based angle of arrival because their oscillators are independent. Arrays such as ESPARGOS need a shared clock for exactly this reason ([arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)).

## Airborne relay is fielded at every tier, and one precedent combines it with sensing

The "comms centre in the sky" is not speculative. Real systems exist at strategic, operational and tactical scale. TurretX belongs in the tactical tier, where demand has grown fastest since 2024.

The Gray Eagle demonstration is the strongest single precedent for the jury. It shows that sensing and relay on one airframe has been done in practice, not just proposed. Relay is also a primary mission in its own right: Kolibri and the RSI kit are relay-only drones. **A TurretX drone that has lost its camera is therefore a fully useful asset, not a failed sensor.** Civil deployments make the concept easy for non-specialists. AT&T's tethered Flying COW gave **about 10 square miles of 5G from about 300 ft** ([EdgeIR](https://edgeir.com/when-cows-fly-att-transmits-5g-from-aerial-drone-in-claimed-industry-first-20220607)). Loon's balloons in hurricane-hit Puerto Rico delivered only texting, email and basic web ([Fox News/AP](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/2017/10/20/google-parent-alphabet-turns-on-internet-balloons-in-puerto-rico.html)). Loon is evidence that **low-bandwidth messaging is the useful baseline in denied areas**, which is exactly what ESP-class radios can carry. The academic grounding is mainstream: Zeng, Zhang and Lim's 2016 IEEE Communications Magazine paper highlights short-range line-of-sight links as the core advantage of low-altitude UAVs ([arXiv 1602.03602](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03602)).

India's record leaves an opening. The Army has bought 130 tethered surveillance drones ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/indian-army-enhancing-operational-preparedness)), iDEX awarded a tethered surveillance-drone challenge ([iDEX notice](https://idex.gov.in/uploads/notice/13-03-24.pdf)), and after Operation Sindoor the forces sought jam-resistant, GPS-free drone comms ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/post-operation-sindoor-armed-forces-look-at-next-gen-tech-on-drones/amp)). Only an unsourced IDRW report mentions trials of a relay payload ([IDRW](https://idrw.org/?p=391576)). The Army's Battlefield Management System, which was meant to provide blue-force tracking, was reportedly **foreclosed in 2017 over cost** ([Indian Defence Review](https://indiandefencereview.com/battlefield-management-system-for-indian-army-where-are-we/)). No public source shows a small-UAV relay node for ground troops combined with authenticated friendly tracking. Post-2018 status is unknown, so the team should say "we found no public program" rather than "none exists."

## Altitude helps friendlies but exposes the drone, so stack the defences

The physics favours an elevated relay, with one catch. On the ground, radio paths follow two-ray loss of L = 120 + 40·log₁₀(d_km) − 20·log₁₀(h_t) − 20·log₁₀(h_r). **Raising an antenna tenfold cuts loss by 20 dB** ([JED](https://www.jedonline.com/2022/08/17/jamming-efficacy-of-communications-links/)). Plugging in the numbers gives an illustrative case. Two handhelds at 1.5 m height and 3 km apart lose about 132 dB. Each hop through a relay at 100 m altitude, 1.5 km from each unit, loses roughly 100 dB at free space. **That leaves each friendly-to-relay hop roughly 30 dB or more stronger than the direct ground link.** A ground jammer has to overcome that margin.

The catch is in the same EW literature: "airborne terminals within line of sight of the jammer have the most severe jamming environment," while ground terminals benefit from terrain masking ([RF Cafe EW handbook](https://rfcafe.com/references/electrical/ew-radar-handbook/jamming-to-signal-ratio-constant-power.htm)). The three-node J/S equation shows where the defender can still win ([RF Wireless World](https://www.rfwireless-world.com/terminology/rf-frequency-jamming-techniques)):

- **Geometry.** Keep the friendly hop short relative to the jammer range, through the Rtr²/Rjr² term.
- **Antenna gain.** Point the antenna down and away from a distant, low-elevation jammer, through the Gjr term.
- **Bandwidth.** Force the jammer to spread its power, through the Br/Bj term.

JED's burn-through worked example shows how much distance matters. Moving a jammer from 3 km to 5 km grows the friendly "parity distance" from 1.886 km to 3.143 km ([JED](https://www.jedonline.com/2022/08/17/jamming-efficacy-of-communications-links/)). **Altitude is a multiplier on other techniques, not a solution by itself.** Saying so up front pre-empts the sharpest juror objection.

The relay must also be **regenerative**: it must decode, authenticate and re-encode each message rather than amplify whatever it hears. That breaks jammer carry-through, drops spoofed traffic and lets the downlink use a different channel or medium. Applied to TurretX, the anti-jam stack looks like this:

| Technique | Fit for the 24 h build | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| HMAC auth plus replay counter | Build it. Reuses the IFF keys, so spoofed or replayed relay traffic is dropped | ESP-NOW docs advise app-level ACKs and sequence numbers; broadcast cannot be encrypted ([ESP-IDF ESP-NOW](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/network/esp_now.html)) |
| Jamming detection (packet delivery ratio vs RSSI) | Build it | High signal strength with low delivery means jamming; delivery ratio alone is ambiguous ([Xu et al. 2005](https://www.cs.sjtu.edu.cn/~wang-xb/wireless_new/coursePages/courseMaterial/UTEXAS_CS395T_Wireless_Networking/40.pdf)) |
| Keyed adaptive channel hopping | Build it, with a rendezvous channel | An ESP-NOW TDMA component already hops channels 1/6/11 based on packet error rate ([Espressif registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/yangcong-bit/esp-now-tdma/versions/1.6.0/readme)) |
| Store-and-forward queue | Build it | Message-ferrying (data-mule) research for partitioned networks ([Zhao et al.](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/fall2009/cmsc828r/PAPERS/ferry.pdf)) |
| Optical fallback (laser/LED) | Build one channel | Ukraine's fibre-optic FPV drones show that non-RF links defeat EW ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/04/5/7506171/)); the UK is funding jam-resistant free-space optical links for UAS ([Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/newsletters/uk-future-capability-group-pursues-free-space-optical-comms-for-uas)) |
| LoRa chirp spread spectrum | Skip unless modules are on hand | Receives about 20 dB below the noise floor ([Semtech](https://blog.semtech.com/long-range-with-lora)), but reactive jamming defeats it with a few symbols ([arXiv 2501.18339](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18339v2)) |
| Null-steering antenna arrays (CRPA), true FHSS/DSSS | Slide only | 4-element arrays reach about 40 dB suppression ([everythingRF](https://www.everythingrf.com/products/anti-jam-solutions/st-engineering/944-1966-gnss-anti-jam-antenna)) |

Emitting has a cost that should be said out loud. Ukrainian drones have located and struck Russian ground internet relay antennas ([Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/4122219-ukrainian-drones-strike-russian-ew-systems-on-southern-slobozhanshchyna-front.html)). A continuously transmitting relay is therefore a direction-finding target. The mitigations are burst transmission, low duty cycle, directional antennas, and a mesh of nodes so that no single relay is a single point of failure.

## A 15–23 hour build fits one developer if jamming is simulated

**Real jamming is off the table.** Under the Indian Wireless Telegraphy Act 1933 and DoT advisories, private individuals and organisations cannot procure or use jammers ([DoT advisory, 2022](https://dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/Advisory%20on%20use%20of%20jammer%20and%20booster.pdf?download=1)). The demo should use a dashboard "jam channel X" control. Nodes receive that flag and drop or corrupt packets on the chosen channel. The demo should add physical blocking with a metal sheet and reduced TX power, so the direct ground link genuinely fails while the overhead relay keeps line of sight. At 2.4 GHz over a 3 m arena, the first Fresnel zone is only about 0.31 m wide, so a floor-level barrier really does block the direct path (calculated from [Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/physics/fresnel-zone)).

ESP-NOW is the right backbone. It carries 1470-byte v2 payloads with built-in CCMP encryption ([ESP-IDF](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/network/esp_now.html)). Espressif measured latency under 20 ms to 300 m outdoors, and the long-range mode reaches 900 m ([Espressif blog](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/esp-now-for-outdoor-applications/)). For the optical channel, the camera-decoded LED blink is lower risk than the laser because it reuses the existing vision pipeline and needs no alignment. At 30 fps with one bit per frame, simple on-off keying yields roughly **10–15 bps**. A 32-bit "unit ID + status + truncated MAC" message takes 3–4 s. That suits "I'm unit 3, OK" under full RF denial, not chat ([Laser Focus World](https://www.laserfocusworld.com/software-accessories/software/article/16548192/algorithms-optimize-nonlineofsight-optical-wireless-data-sent-to-smartphones)). A laser into a photodiode and comparator into an ESP32 UART is far faster: hobby builds report hundreds of bps to hundreds of kbps ([EFY](https://www.electronicsforu.com/electronics-projects/li-fi-laser-communication)). It should be a fixed relay-to-base link, and only if the hardware team has it working by about hour 12.

| Build item (hour figures are estimates) | Hours | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| ESP-NOW firmware for unit A, unit B, relay and gateway, with sequence/ACK/retransmit | 3–4 | Low |
| HMAC-SHA256 plus replay counter, with a reject log | 1.5–2 | Low |
| Relay store-and-forward queue with TTL and dedup | 1.5–2 | Low |
| Simulated jam control plus delivery-ratio/RSSI detector | 2 | Low–Med |
| Keyed channel hopping plus rendezvous resync | 2–3 | Med (peers lose sync) |
| Laptop dashboard over gateway serial | 3–4 | Low |
| Camera-health watchdog plus L0–L4 ladder plus RF-only BFT view | 2–3 | Low |
| Camera-decoded LED blink uplink | 3–4 | Med (exposure, dropped frames) |
| Laser relay-to-base link (optional) | 1–2 software, 2–3 hardware | Med–High (alignment) |
| Servo-swept RF bearing check (optional) | 4–6 | Med (multipath) |

The cut line is the first seven rows, about 15–20 hours, plus the LED optical link if time allows. The demo runs as three scenes:

1. **The camera dies, and TurretX keeps working.** Cover the camera. Within half a second the dashboard drops to L2: the laser LED goes dark, friendlies stay plotted with beacon age, and a banner reads "non-emitters not visible." Uncover the camera; the laser stays safed until the operator re-confirms.
2. **The relay restores comms.** Cars A and B are behind a barrier. The direct delivery ratio drops to zero. The relay carries traffic. A replayed packet from a "red" node is rejected on screen.
3. **Jam and adapt.** Press "jam ch 6." The detector trips, nodes hop channels and the link recovers. Then press "jam all RF." The LED blink path carries status, and queued messages flush when RF returns.

Pre-record each scene as a backup video, lock the camera exposure, and pick the quietest venue channel during setup.

## The pitch reframes the camera question as a mission-continuity answer

The answer to "what if the camera fails?" should run in three beats, each tied to a precedent the jurors will recognise:

1. **Safety first.** "Losing the camera never makes us more dangerous. The laser fails safe and requires fresh operator confirmation, exactly the 'terminate or obtain operator input' rule in DoDD 3000.09, and the lesson of the Patriot–Tornado fratricide, where a silent IFF failure plus too much autonomy killed a friendly crew."
2. **The picture degrades instead of vanishing.** "We drop to an authenticated blue-force picture, like BFT but at 5 Hz instead of ~10 s, and we tell the operator what we can't see."
3. **The drone changes mission.** "The aircraft is still overhead, so it becomes a relay. That is what Gray Eagle demonstrated in 2024 and what Ukraine's Kolibri and repeater drones do every day. Because the relay checks the same HMAC keys as our IFF, it forwards only authenticated friendlies."

Then show Scene 1 live.

Jurors from a drone manufacturer will probe endurance, weight and emissions, so concede those before they are raised. A relay radio adds weight and power draw. The Vidlunnia repeater runs about 60 minutes on a DJI Matrice 300 ([Rubryka](https://rubryka.com/en/2024/06/13/retranslyator-vidlunnya-dlya-droniv/)), and tethered systems trade mobility for endurance. The emitting drone becomes a target. One relay is a single point of failure, which is why fielded designs auto-return on link loss and why production systems use meshes of Silvus, Persistent or Doodle Labs radios, the last advertising band-switching against jamming ([Doodle Labs](https://doodlelabs.com/products/dual-radio)).

Avoid three overclaims. LoRa is not "unjammable." The RSSI bearing check is not cryptographic distance bounding. The arena demonstrates **simulated** jamming, not real EW resistance. Naming the production path shows the team knows the difference between a prototype and a system: FTM or UWB ranging, CRPA nulling, MANET radios, and a thermal camera as a sensor of a different physics ([arXiv 2401.10837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.10837)).

## Conclusion

Aerion's question has a better answer than "we added a backup camera." Camera loss can trigger a **planned mission change** rather than a fault. Doctrine already separates fail-safe weapons from fail-soft awareness, and the relay role is independent of the EO payload. A drone without its camera therefore keeps doing two useful jobs: authenticated blue-force tracking and an authenticated relay. TurretX also has one feature fielded BFT and relay products do not combine. The **same cryptographic identity** decides who counts as friendly for targeting, which traffic the relay forwards, and, through the friendly-as-calibration check, whether the camera can be trusted. That shared trust anchor is the most defensible novelty in the pitch.

The relay claim stands on fielded systems from the USAF, US Army and Ukraine, while the anti-jam claim depends on layering. Altitude alone helps the jammer as much as the defender. Present it as "line of sight plus regeneration plus agility plus an optical fallback," demo it with legal simulated jamming, and say where the gaps are. In front of a defence jury, that honesty is a feature, because it is the same discipline that separates TurretX's design from the Patriot failure it is built to avoid.
